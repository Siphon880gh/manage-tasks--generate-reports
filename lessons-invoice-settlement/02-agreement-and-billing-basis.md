# Lesson 02 — Start With the Agreement and Billing Basis

Settlement begins before delivery. If the commercial rule is ambiguous, perfect time logs cannot make the final claim unambiguous.

## Capture the minimum commercial facts

For every engagement, identify:

- legal parties and billing contacts;
- contract, statement of work, or purchase-order reference;
- scope and exclusions;
- start/end dates and billing cadence;
- currency, tax treatment, and payment terms;
- billing basis: time and materials, fixed fee, milestone, retainer, usage, reimbursement, or non-cash exchange;
- rates, fee schedule, caps, minimums, discounts, deposits, and retainage;
- acceptance criteria and approvers;
- change-control method; and
- submission channel and required attachments.

Write down assumptions. “Monthly support” is not a calculation rule until the parties know whether unused capacity expires, rolls over, or is refundable, and whether out-of-scope hours are separately billable.

## Convert prose into testable billing rules

Use a compact billing memo:

> Bill monthly in USD. The $6,000 retainer covers up to 40 hours of listed services. Additional pre-approved hours are $175/hour. Submit by the third business day with a deliverable schedule. Customer approval is due within five business days; payment is net 30 from a valid invoice.

Each sentence now produces a check: correct period, correct base fee, capacity comparison, evidence of pre-approval, applicable rate, timely submission, and due-date calculation.

## Keep identifiers stable

The example workbook uses an ID and a structured key for each line. That pattern is valuable even if the customer never sees the internal ID. A stable key lets reviewers trace a line across task systems, exported schedules, invoice revisions, and payment conversations. Do not reuse a key for unrelated work.

## Control scope changes

When scope changes, record who requested it, the date, the price/time effect, and who approved it. Link the approval to the affected line. A chat message can be supporting evidence, but summarize the decision so a future reviewer does not have to infer it from a long thread.

Never silently change the billing rule after work is performed. If the parties agree to a concession, show it as a documented adjustment or credit. This preserves the original economics and the reason for the final amount.

## Before work enters settlement

Confirm that the work is attached to the right project and engagement. In LedgerLane, engagement terms may be one-time, retainer, barter, or community partnership. Set the arrangement and amount deliberately: a barter or community arrangement with a positive cash amount is treated as having a cash component, while one without an amount can be presented as non-cash.
