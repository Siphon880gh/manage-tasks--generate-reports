# Lesson 08 — Settle Cash, Retainer, and Fixed-Fee Arrangements

## Time and materials

For time-and-materials work, preserve approved rates, billable time, and any expense rules. The schedule should show sufficient detail for the customer to validate quantity without exposing irrelevant internal commentary. Reconcile submitted hours to source records, remove non-billable work, and document rounding.

## Fixed fee and milestones

A fixed fee is earned and billed according to the agreed trigger—not automatically according to hours spent. Typical triggers include signing, delivery, customer acceptance, or a calendar date. Show the milestone, its status, the agreed fee, and acceptance evidence. Track hours internally to understand profitability, but do not let overrun alter the customer price without an approved change.

Deposits and advance billings require special care: distinguish cash received from work accepted, and follow the applicable accounting policy for revenue recognition. A settlement report can document the commercial event but does not determine accounting treatment.

## Retainers

Retainers can mean different things:

- a recurring fee for access or availability;
- prepaid hours drawn down as used;
- a minimum monthly commitment;
- a recurring package of deliverables; or
- an advance held against future invoices.

State which model applies. Reconcile opening balance, additions, usage, expiration or rollover, adjustments, and closing balance when a retainer represents a bank of value. For a recurring service fee, list the period and promised services even if no hourly reconciliation is contractually required.

## One-time settlements

For a single settlement, connect the accepted quote, delivered scope, final amount, and release or closure terms. Confirm whether “final payment” settles only the listed invoice or all claims under the engagement. Do not imply a legal release unless the parties actually agreed to one.

## Cash controls

Before issue, verify currency and beneficiary details using an approved, independent channel—especially after any request to change bank information. Restrict who can alter payment instructions. Separate preparation from approval where practical.

After receipt, match the exact amount, payer, currency, value date, invoice reference, and remittance advice. Treat bank fees, withholding, foreign-exchange differences, and partial payment as reconciling items rather than silently marking the whole invoice paid.
