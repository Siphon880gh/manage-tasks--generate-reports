# Lesson 04 — Design Clear Deliverable Lines

A good settlement line is specific enough to validate, brief enough to scan, and phrased in language the customer recognizes.

## Separate internal and client-facing names

An internal task might be “M06-OCT-2 metadata fixes,” while the customer-facing line is “Metadata and structured-data implementation.” Keep the stable internal key for traceability, but use a clear client-facing title on the report. LedgerLane's **Client-facing name** does this without renaming the operational task.

Avoid vague lines such as “miscellaneous,” “support,” or “work completed.” Prefer an outcome, artifact, or bounded service:

- “Website launch planning and roadmap”
- “Keyword universe with intent tagging”
- “September onsite installation support”

## Use parent and sub-lines deliberately

Use a parent when several details form one commercial deliverable. Use sub-lines to explain components, evidence, or hours. For example:

- **Content strategy implementation**
  - Keyword research dataset — 3 hours
  - Funnel-stage mapping — 4 hours
  - Publishing schedule — 2 hours

Do not put 9 hours on the parent and repeat 3, 4, and 2 on its children in a manual total. That produces 18 if someone sums every visible number. Choose one convention:

- parent is a calculated total and children carry quantities; or
- parent carries the billed quantity and children are descriptive only.

LedgerLane rolls immediate child hours into the parent display while also displaying the child rows. Its summary statistic adds the hours of all displayed rows, so parent/child structures require a deliberate reconciliation before external use. Export the CSV and confirm that your chosen presentation does not imply double billing.

## Decide what belongs on the report

LedgerLane includes work from every status unless a task is explicitly excluded. This supports advance, milestone, and mixed-status settlement, but it means “on the report” is not proof of completion. Review each line against the agreement and acceptance rule. Exclude internal administration, duplicated tasks, unapproved work, and items outside the cut-off unless they are legitimately billable.

## Preserve ownership and period

An owner helps route questions; the billing period assigns the work to a settlement cycle. Neither field alone proves delivery. If several people contribute, choose the accountable owner and use evidence or a note for contributors rather than multiplying invoice lines merely to list staff.

## Quality test

A reviewer should be able to read a line and answer: “What did the customer receive, under which agreement, in which period, for what quantity or price, and where is the proof?” If not, revise the structure.
