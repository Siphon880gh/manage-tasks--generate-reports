# Lesson 03 — Choose the Right Documentation Level

Documentation should be proportionate to value, risk, novelty, and customer requirements. “Lightweight” does not mean undocumented; “audit-ready” does not mean attaching every internal message.

## Level 1: Basic settlement

**Use when:** low-value, routine work for a trusted customer under simple terms.

**Typical pack:**

- agreement or accepted quote;
- one clear line per deliverable or period;
- billing period and amount;
- a completion or acceptance reference; and
- invoice plus proof of delivery and payment.

The reviewer should still be able to identify the parties, obligation, calculation, approval, and receipt. A single evidence link per line may be enough.

## Level 2: Supported settlement

**Use when:** work spans several deliverables or months, is time-based, has multiple approvers, or is likely to attract questions.

Add:

- parent deliverables with detailed sub-lines;
- hours by billing period;
- named evidence links to outputs, meeting records, or approvals;
- rate and amount reconciliation;
- change approvals; and
- an internal review checklist.

The example workbook fits this level: it separates logged hours into monthly columns and pairs evidence names with URLs. Parentheses distinguish detail hours beneath summary rows. The goal is to show composition without double counting.

## Level 3: Controlled or audit-ready settlement

**Use when:** value or regulatory exposure is high, public/grant funds are involved, the work is disputed, related parties are involved, or contractual controls are strict.

Add:

- versioned contracts, purchase orders, and amendments;
- immutable or access-controlled source records;
- formal acceptance by an authorized person;
- preparer/reviewer approval and segregation of duties;
- detailed rate-card, tax, expense, and foreign-exchange support;
- invoice/revision/credit-note history;
- bank or remittance matching; and
- documented retention and access rules.

Level 3 is a control environment, not just a bigger attachment folder.

## How to select a level

Raise the level when any of these increase: monetary value, customization, judgment in the calculation, evidence volatility, number of handoffs, customer procurement requirements, tax complexity, dispute probability, or consequences of error. The customer's required format always establishes a floor.

## Hybrid approach

Apply a baseline to the whole invoice and enhanced support only to risky lines. For example, routine monthly reporting may remain Level 1 while an out-of-scope implementation uses Level 2 and a reimbursable international expense uses Level 3 controls.

Document the selected level and exceptions. A short decision such as “Level 2; formal acceptance additionally required for milestone 4” is more useful than an unwritten habit.
