# Lesson 11 — Reconcile Payment and Close the Record

Issuing the invoice creates an open item; it does not close the transaction.

## Match the receipt

For a cash settlement, compare:

- customer/payer identity;
- invoice and remittance references;
- currency and gross amount;
- value date;
- bank fees or payment-processor fees;
- withholding or deductions;
- credits applied; and
- amount remaining open.

A payment can cover several invoices, and one invoice can receive several payments. Allocate using remittance advice and documented rules rather than guessing from equal amounts.

## Handle differences

**Partial payment:** apply only the received amount and keep the balance open.

**Short payment:** identify whether the cause is a fee, withholding, disputed line, credit, or mistake. Obtain supporting documentation before clearing the difference.

**Overpayment:** record it according to policy as an unapplied receipt, credit balance, or refund obligation; do not quietly absorb it.

**Wrong currency or exchange difference:** retain the contractual currency, transaction rate source, settlement amount, and resulting gain/loss treatment as required by accounting policy.

**Unidentified receipt:** hold it in the appropriate suspense or unapplied-cash process until identification. Similar value is not sufficient evidence.

## Close criteria

Close the settlement only when:

1. the issued claim and any revisions are preserved;
2. approval and delivery are evidenced;
3. cash or non-cash consideration is fully matched, or an authorized adjustment resolves the balance;
4. disputes and credits are linked;
5. the open balance is zero for a valid reason; and
6. records are retained under the applicable policy.

Marking a task done or an invoice paid is not enough if a credit, withholding certificate, or second side of a barter exchange remains outstanding.

## The close packet

Retain the agreement and changes, final settlement schedule, formal invoice or non-cash record, evidence index, acceptance, issue/delivery record, correspondence on exceptions, credits/revisions, remittance or reciprocal-performance proof, and reconciliation. Use access controls appropriate to financial and customer data.

Finally, record lessons for the next cycle: late evidence, recurring customer rejection codes, rate discrepancies, or unclear lines. Settlement is complete when the balance is resolved **and** the reasoning can be reconstructed.
