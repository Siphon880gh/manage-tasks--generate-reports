# Lesson 01 — What Invoice Settlement Means

## The four events people often confuse

An **invoice** is a request or formal record of value due. **Approval** is the customer's confirmation that the claim is valid and may proceed through accounts payable. **Payment** is the transfer of cash or another agreed consideration. **Settlement** is the broader resolution: both parties agree what extinguishes the obligation, the transfer is matched, exceptions are resolved, and the record can be closed.

An invoice can therefore be issued but not approved, approved but not paid, or paid but not properly reconciled. Calling every one of those states “settled” hides risk.

## A practical lifecycle

1. **Establish the obligation.** Preserve the contract, statement of work, purchase order, approved change, and settlement terms.
2. **Capture performance.** Record deliverables, time, milestones, expenses, dates, owners, and evidence while the work happens.
3. **Cut off a billing period.** Decide which work belongs in the period and which work remains unbilled or deferred.
4. **Calculate the claim.** Apply rates, fixed fees, caps, discounts, taxes, retainage, deposits, and credits as applicable.
5. **Review and approve.** Verify scope, arithmetic, evidence, client identity, payment instructions, and authorization.
6. **Issue and deliver.** Create a uniquely identified document and send it through the customer's required channel.
7. **Resolve exceptions.** Track questions, rejected lines, short payments, credit notes, and revised documents without erasing history.
8. **Receive and match consideration.** Match the bank receipt, remittance, or non-cash performance to the open item.
9. **Close and retain.** Mark the obligation settled only for the matched amount, then preserve the supporting record.

## Three records, three audiences

The **work record** is operational: tasks, notes, owners, time, and links. The **settlement report** explains the claim to the customer: client-facing deliverables, period, evidence, and amount. The **accounting record** tracks the receivable, payment, credits, tax, and ledger treatment. They should reconcile, but they need not be identical documents.

LedgerLane's Invoice settlement report is best understood as the middle record: a settlement-ready schedule of work. It can be printed or exported to CSV and can support a formal invoice, but teams must still add any legally required supplier/customer identity, invoice number, issue date, due date, tax data, currency, and payment instructions in their invoicing or accounting system.

## Core principle

Do not optimize for the largest possible evidence pack. Optimize for a clear chain from **agreement → work → calculation → approval → settlement**. Every additional document should make one of those links more reliable.
