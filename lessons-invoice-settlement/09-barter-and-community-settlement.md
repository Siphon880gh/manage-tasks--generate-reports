# Lesson 09 — Record Barter and Community Settlements

Not every obligation is settled solely with cash. A barter arrangement exchanges goods or services; a community partnership may exchange advocacy, access, sponsorship, promotion, facilities, or other agreed value. These arrangements still need a precise record.

## Define both sides of the exchange

Document:

- what each party must provide;
- quantities, quality standards, and delivery dates;
- the agreed valuation method, if any;
- cash top-ups or reimbursements;
- acceptance and remedy terms;
- tax responsibilities; and
- what event completes settlement.

“Promotion in exchange for consulting” is too vague. Specify channels, number and timing of placements, audience or placement requirements, consulting deliverables, and approval rights.

## Do not fabricate a cash receivable

If no cash is due, use a settlement record rather than a cash invoice demanding payment. LedgerLane supports this distinction: barter or community-partnership engagements with no cash amount can appear as **Non-cash** and show the exchange or arrangement note. If a positive amount is entered, the engagement has a cash component in the report.

Do not enter a nominal cash amount merely to make a non-cash record look complete. Conversely, do not omit a real cash balancing payment.

## Evidence both performances

Settlement is not complete merely because your side delivered. Keep proof for each party's obligation: delivered files, event attendance, publication links, access records, sponsorship placement, sign-off, or other agreed evidence. Record partial performance and remaining obligations explicitly.

## Valuation and tax

Non-cash does not necessarily mean zero value or no tax/accounting consequence. Fair-value measurement, sales or value-added taxes, income recognition, related-party rules, and charitable or sponsorship treatment vary. Preserve the agreed valuation basis and consult qualified finance or tax advisors for the applicable jurisdiction.

## Close-out statement

A useful close-out states:

> Party A delivered [items] on [dates]. Party B delivered [consideration] on [dates]. Exceptions: [none/list]. The parties confirm the listed obligations for [period/scope] are fulfilled, subject to [surviving terms].

This is clearer than marking a cash invoice “paid” when no cash moved.
