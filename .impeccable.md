## Design Context

### Users
Operators, project managers, and finance-adjacent people who turn delivery into proof — invoices, stakeholder pulses, and a board they can stand behind. They work in one browser on one machine. Some accounts only look (viewers). Admins invite; editors move work. They are often interrupted and will not read a wall of chrome.

### Brand Personality
Accounted-for, private, brutal. The interface should feel like a ledger that still has a pulse: confident, local, a little loud on purpose. Emotion: “I can see the work and I can prove it.” Not playful SaaS. Not a dark dashboard with neon.

### Aesthetic Direction
Paper and ink. Hard 2px rules. Offset shadows. Acid lime for the one thing that matters. Blue for emphasis, coral for the person. Manrope for reading, DM Mono for labels that behave like stamps — not for paragraphs.

References in spirit: a stamped invoice, a warehouse stencil, ClickUp only for the *interaction* of a top record bar and a task sheet — never for the look.

Anti-references: Inter-on-white app chrome, cyan-on-dark AI slop, glassmorphism, hero-metric grids, identical icon cards, jammed toolbars.

Light only. No dark theme unless a future brief asks for it.

### Design Principles
1. **Four things, then a door.** At any decision, show ≤4 options. The rest live behind More, details, or a modal.
2. **The work is the page.** Headlines and marketing lines must not push the board or the report below the fold.
3. **Read it at a glance.** Body ≥16px, sentence case, measure under 65ch, contrast ≥4.5:1. Stamps (uppercase mono) are for labels, never for help text.
4. **Say what the control does.** Avatars, icons, and abbreviations get a visible name or a real accessible name that matches the action.
5. **Reveal complexity when someone asks.** Defaults do the common job. Advanced (column type, timestamps, invite, display toggles) starts closed.
